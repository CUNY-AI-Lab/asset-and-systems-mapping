# Classification evidence and course convention

Updated November 5, 2026. This corrects the earlier automatic policy-to-Macro rule and the policy-based Macro test fixtures.

## Primary sources

- Bronfenbrenner, *Toward an Experimental Ecology of Human Development* (1977), p. 515: Macro concerns general cultural/institutional patterns rather than particular contexts. Those patterns can be expressed in recorded laws and rules, as well as implicit ideology and custom. [Primary paper, CUNY-hosted](https://files.commons.gc.cuny.edu/wp-content/blogs.dir/17937/files/2021/08/Bronfenbrenner-1977.pdf).
- Bronfenbrenner, *Ecological Models of Human Development* (1994), sections 3.1–3.5: Micro involves immediate participation; Exo involves an outside setting and an indirect influence on immediate processes; Macro concerns overarching cultural/subcultural patterns, beliefs, resources, customs and opportunity structures. Chronosystem concerns continuity and change through time. [Primary chapter, Columbia-hosted](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment%28short%20version%29.pdf).

Neither a proper noun, the word policy, a national jurisdiction nor application across several sites determines the ecological level. A particular administrative protocol does not establish a cultural pattern. Conversely, the theory does not categorically exclude laws from its account of Macro patterns.

## Explicit course representation

The user clarified: **policies and offices are situated in the Exosphere**. The application therefore represents concrete policies/laws and external administrative offices as Exo nodes, and independently evidenced cultural, ideological, historical and economic conditions as Macro nodes. This is the course's operational representation, not a claim that all ecological literature assigns every policy to Exo.

| Memo evidence | Representation |
|---|---|
| Learners directly meet advisers in a wellbeing room | Micro people and setting |
| Learners do not participate in a named scheduling office's allocation meetings; its decisions reduce appointments | Exo office, with the nonparticipation/indirect-effect chain quoted |
| A named referral consent protocol requires permission before records are shared | Exo policy, with its operative rule quoted |
| A documented cultural expectation of self-reliance discourages seeking support | Macro belief/cultural condition |
| A documented history of unequal investment limits services in some neighborhoods | Macro historical/structural condition; mere age is insufficient |
| Schools need funding; government should help | No invented policy, office or Macro condition |

A policy is not a setting: do not require learners to be described as absent from a policy. Its evidence must describe a specific established operative rule in the focal situation. A named office still requires the participation test; if people directly participate there it may belong at Micro. Never invent an office to explain a policy or an ideology to explain an office. Students author every relationship.

## Implementation and compatibility

The account submission prompt, generated schema, validation and classification menu share this convention. New automatic Macro policy/law entries are rejected, including obvious policy labels disguised as a different type. Exo accepts policies and laws as well as identified outside settings/resources. Macro accepts beliefs, economic conditions, history, culture and structures. Existing projects and exact identity/evidence updates remain readable; saved nodes, manual corrections, notes, colors and connections are not migrated or silently rewritten. Students can explicitly move an earlier policy node to Exo.

The earlier fictional cross-site allocation/consent rules were inadequate Macro examples. Their historical model-test scores demonstrate compliance with the old rubric, not correct Macro classification under this corrected rubric. New examples separate administrative rules from independently evidenced cultural patterns. Finite examples and conservative guards do not guarantee flawless semantic classification.
